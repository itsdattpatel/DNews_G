## 1. Compliance Principles (Rule G8)

1. **Zero Proprietary/Unverified Material**: Only texts, data, and translations that carry an explicitly verified Public Domain dedication or recognized Open License (CC BY 4.0, CC BY-SA 4.0, CC0) are permitted.
2. **On-Screen Attribution**: Every single historical record, verse, quote, and psychological card must render an immutable provenance badge citing:
   - Source repository / book title
   - Author / Translator
   - Publication year
   - Exact license identifier
   - Direct link to the source document / digital surrogate
3. **No Paraphrasing History as Fact**: Historical news clippings are presented verbatim from archival feeds or transcripts, preserving historical records without speculative AI alterations.

## 3. Sacred Scriptures Directory (Rule B4)

### 3.1 Initial Core Registry

#### 1. The Bhagavad Gita
- **Sanskrit Source**: Mahabharata Bhīṣma Parva (Chapters 23–40). Public domain recension hosted on Wikisource and GRETIL (Goettingen Register of Electronic Texts in Indian Languages).
- **English Translation**: Sir Edwin Arnold, *The Song Celestial; or, Bhagavad-Gîtâ (from the Mahâbhârata)*. First published 1885 (London: Trübner & Co.).
  - **License**: Public Domain worldwide (Author died 1904).
  - **Digital Repository**: Project Gutenberg EBook #2388 (`https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2388`).
  - **Attribution Display**: *"Translated by Sir Edwin Arnold (1885). Public Domain."*

#### 2. The Mahabharata
- **Sanskrit Source**: Poona Critical Edition recension extracts / Ganguli Sanskrit baseline (Public Domain).
- **English Translation**: Kisari Mohan Ganguli, *The Mahabharata of Krishna-Dwaipayana Vyasa translated into English Prose*. Published between 1883 and 1896 (Calcutta: Bharata Press / Protap Chundra Roy).
  - **License**: Public Domain worldwide (Translator died pre-1920).
  - **Digital Repository**: Sacred Texts Archive (`https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/`) and Wikisource.
  - **Attribution Display**: *"Translated by Kisari Mohan Ganguli (1883–1896). Public Domain."*

#### 3. The Ramayana (Valmiki)
- **Sanskrit Source**: Valmiki Ramayana public domain recensions (7 Kandas: Bala, Ayodhya, Aranya, Kishkindha, Sundara, Yuddha, Uttara).
- **English Translations**:
  - Ralph T. H. Griffith, *The Rámáyan of Válmíki translated into English Verse* (1870–1874).
  - Manmatha Nath Dutt, *The Ramayana: Translated into English Prose from the Original Sanskrit of Valmiki* (1891–1894).
  - **License**: Public Domain worldwide.
  - **Digital Repository**: Sacred Texts Archive (`https://sacred-texts.com/hin/rama/`) and Project Gutenberg.
  - **Attribution Display**: *"Translated by Ralph T. H. Griffith (1870–1874) / M. N. Dutt (1891). Public Domain."*

### 3.2 Candidate Holy Books for Registry Expansion (For User Approval)
- **The Dhammapada** (Buddhist Canonical Scripture): Translation by Friedrich Max Müller, *The Sacred Books of the East, Vol. X* (1881, Oxford University Press). Public domain.
- **The Tao Te Ching** (Daoist Canonical Text by Laozi): Translation by James Legge, *The Sacred Books of the East, Vol. XXXIX* (1891) or Lionel Giles, *The Sayings of Lao Tzu* (1904). Public domain.
- **The Principal Upanishads**: Translation by Swami Paramananda (1919, Boston) or F. Max Müller (1879, Oxford). Public domain.
- **The Bible (King James Version, 1611)**: Public domain worldwide (with traditional crown copyright restrictions in the UK inapplicable to open non-commercial software).
